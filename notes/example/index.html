Keeping strategy in use

Most product strategies fail quietly. Nobody argues with them; they simply stop being used. The document is approved, the roadmap is drawn from it once, and within a quarter the team is making decisions the way it always did: by whoever asks loudest, or by whatever shipped last.

A practical strategy is one that keeps getting used. It helps someone decide what to do on an ordinary Tuesday, not just what to say at the offsite.

Start with the choice, not the vision

A vision describes where you want to be. A strategy describes what you will give up to get there. If a plan does not make at least one reasonable request harder to say yes to, it is not yet a strategy.

The useful test is simple. Take the last five things the team was asked to build and hold each one up against the strategy. If the strategy cannot tell you which to decline, it needs sharpening.

  • Name the customer you are choosing to serve first.
  • Name the problem you will solve better than anyone.
  • Name the work you will stop, even though it is valuable.

Make it small enough to remember

If people have to open a document to recall the strategy, it will not shape their decisions. The best versions fit in a paragraph and survive being repeated badly in a corridor.

Strategy is the set of choices you would still defend when the easy option is sitting in front of you.

Write it down, then say it until you are tired of hearing it. That is usually about the point where the rest of the organisation starts to hear it for the first time.

Review it against reality

Every few months, look at what actually shipped and ask whether it followed from the strategy or happened in spite of it. The gap between the two is the most honest measure of whether the strategy is working, and it is often the best place to find the next version.
